# Australia sources (read 21 Oct 2026)
Australia is NOT in the OWID file. Values were transcribed by hand into data/processed/australia_aemo.csv.
PRIMARY (official AEMO sources):
  - AEMO, "Digital demand surge", 1 Jun 2026 - https://www.aemo.com.au/newsroom/news-updates/digital-demand-surge
    Used: 162 operational data centres nationally; about 2% of today’s grid-supplied electricity use.
  - AEMO, 2026 Electricity Statement of Opportunities (ESOO), 25 Aug 2026 - https://www.aemo.com.au/newsroom/media-release/2026-esoo
    Used: data centre electricity consumption in the NEM forecast to rise from approximately 5 TWh in 2025-26 to 34 TWh by 2035-36, or around 3% to 13% of grid-supplied electricity.
  - AEMO, 2025 Inputs, Assumptions and Scenarios Report (IASR), final report - https://aemo.com.au/-/media/files/stakeholder_consultation/consultations/nem-consultations/2024/2025-iasr-scenarios/final-docs/2025-inputs-assumptions-and-scenarios-report.pdf?la=en
    Used: historical baseline of about 4 TWh in 2024-25; Step Change forecast of roughly 12 TWh by 2030 and about 34 TWh by 2050 (around 6% and 12% of grid-supplied electricity).
SECONDARY (not used for the chart unless clearly labelled):
  - Climate Council - https://www.climatecouncil.org.au/what-does-the-data-centre-boom-mean-for-australias-switch-to-renewables/ (2024-25 about 4 TWh, 2%)
  - Tech Insider - https://tech-insider.org/au/australia-data-centre-boom-power-bill-2026/ (3.9 TWh in 2024-25)
  - dig.watch - https://dig.watch/updates/australia-data-centre-electricity-demand (2026 ESOO figures reported in the media)
  - Data Centre Magazine - https://datacentremagazine.com/news/australian-data-centres-aemo-2026-electricity-statement (52 TWh high-growth)
  - W.Media - https://w.media/aemo-data-centres-could-account-for-almost-10-of-nem-electricity-demand-by-2050/ (2026 ISP; different denominator basis)
Important caveat: Australian figures are NEM/grid-supplied, financial-year values, and AEMO estimates/forecasts. They are not like-for-like with OWID global shares and must not be treated as proof of local grid congestion.
